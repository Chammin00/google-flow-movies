## 1. Series Logline & Y2K Stakes

In rain-drenched **December 1999**, six days before Tokyo's midnight transition into the year 2000, the shadowy continuity syndicate known as **The Archivist Bureau** prepares to execute the city-wide **Y2K Analog-to-Digital Broadcast Cutover (`1999.12.31 → 2000.01.01`)**.

Officially, the cutover is marketed as a harmless telecom modernization. In truth, every analog frequency carries a **13.000-second magnetic hysteresis buffer**—a real-time echo before events lock into history. The Bureau uses **Channel 13**, an unindexed dead band inside that 13-second gap, as a dumping ground for erased people, scrapped blocks, and inconvenient timelines. Switching Tokyo onto sterile digital carriers at midnight on January 1st will permanently purge Channel 13—and annihilate everyone trapped inside dead air.

Basement electronics engineer **Toma Kurose** builds a jury-rigged vacuum-tube **Phase-Tape transceiver** to rewind `13` seconds from the night his sister **Yuki** vanished. When his wrist counter surges past the hard physical limit into **`13.001`**, Channel 13 cracks open across Tokyo—anchoring deleted street brawler **Renji Takanashi** back into the rain, triggering a Bureau Purge order led by elite Sweeper **Masato Kurosawa**, and drawing rogue swordsman **Kuroha ("Vesper")** out of the dark.

## 2. Channel 13 Mythology & Power Rules (Hard Tradeoffs)

### A. The `13.000s` Hysteresis Buffer
- Reality commits like analog magnetic tape: for **`13.000` seconds**, any movement, strike, or fatal blow sits in wet phosphor before setting.
- Standard **Phase-Tape** operation loops inside `0.000–13.000s`: Toma can inspect residual echoes, replay radio fragments (`Sora`'s telemetry), or snap physical inertia back inside a strict 13-second window.

### B. The `13.001s` Rule Break & Overclock (`13.130s`)
- Pushing a Phase-Tape beyond **`13.001s`** breaks continuity lock completely. Instead of merely rewinding the last 13 seconds, the Tuner can **graft erased moments or unchosen alternate futures directly into the present**.
- **Two Unforgiving Tradeoffs:**
  1. **City-Wide Beacon:** Every breach past `13.001s` floods Tokyo's analog spectrum with an unmistakable crimson-amber spike, letting both the Archivist Bureau and Kuroha pinpoint the Tuner's exact block.
  2. **Anchor Erosion:** Dead-Channel current burns away pieces of the Tuner's own present-day timeline (`Toma` risks fading into static like `Renji`; overuse turns human memory into unreadable tape snow).

## 4. Visual & Musical Motif Lock

- **Visual Craft Standard (1990s Hand-Inked Cel Anti-Slop):**
  - Heavy hand-inked contours, wet-asphalt specular reflections, gouache storm clouds, 35mm grain; **zero 3D CGI or glossy plastic shading**.
  - **Color Language:** Warm **amber vacuum-tube glow** (Toma's workshop / hope) vs. cold **cyan CRT scanlines** (Channel 13 ghosts / Sora radio) vs. **obsidian-violet *Dead-Fire*** (Kuroha) vs. stark *Hakukoma*/*Kurokoma* B&W & duotone impact frames (`dandadan_cyan` / `dandadan_crimson`).
- **Audio & Score Signature:**
  - **Toma / Shop:** Warm Dm9 Rhodes chords + physical brass Nixie ticks (`620 Hz / 1420 Hz`) + pattering window rain.
  - **Masato:** Low `49 Hz` sub-bass metronome pulse + **pre-impact vacuum silence cutouts (`0.20s–0.22s`)** before finger snaps.
  - **Renji:** `126–148 BPM` distorted analog bass + punchy snare/kick breaks + electric blue static snaps.
  - **Sora:** Bandpass-filtered (`360–3400 Hz`) radio callouts cutting sharply through combat noise.
  - **Kuroha:** Tritone sub-pulse + roaring flame hiss + deep *Kurokoma* purple blade strikes.
